\subsection{Research Gap}
\label{sec:research_gap}

Recent literature on autonomous quadcopter navigation in GNSS-denied environments reveals a pronounced divide between computational hardware requirements, localization accuracy, and full 3D autonomy. On the high-compute end, state-of-the-art visual-inertial navigation and planning frameworks rely on desktop-class x86 processors or CUDA-enabled embedded GPUs to achieve agile 3D flight. Using Intel Core i7 NUC platforms, Mohta et al.~\cite{mohta2018} demonstrated stereo-VIO (SVO+UKF) with quadratic-programming safe corridors at speeds up to $8.0~\text{m/s}$ ($<0.60~\text{m}$ drift over $60~\text{m}$ at $\sim 80\%$ CPU load), Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2021} introduced ESDF-free B-spline replanning (EGO-Planner) at $0.81~\text{ms}$ planning latency without reporting onboard VIO-coupled trajectory RMSE, and Hu et al.~\cite{hu2025} evaluated VINS-Mono with EGO-Planner in dense indoor clutter ($\ge 70\%$ success rate) while noting that monocular VIO drift induced false virtual obstacles. Similarly, deployments on NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX modules achieve robust 3D navigation in unstructured forests and multi-agent clutter, yet real-world visual-inertial localization errors still reach $0.33\text{--}0.47~\text{m}$ ATE under VINS-Fusion and EGO-Planner-v2~\cite{karjalainen2025}, $0.51~\text{m}$ simulated SLAM ATE ($<1.0~\text{m}$ real terminal drift) under OKVIS2~\cite{barbaslaina2025}, or $3.31~\text{m}$ closed-loop VIO drift under collaborative neural planning~\cite{swift2025}. While multi-modal LiDAR-inertial-visual pipelines such as FAST-LIVO2~\cite{zheng2024} tighten ATE RMSE to $0.045\text{--}0.080~\text{m}$, they depend on costly, power-intensive 3D LiDAR payloads alongside Intel i7 or Jetson Orin NX hardware.

Conversely, existing navigation systems deployed on low-cost, resource-constrained single-board computers have been forced to compromise dimensionality, flight speed, or sensor minimalism. On a GPU-less Raspberry Pi 4B, Shah et al.~\cite{shah2025} demonstrated indoor visual SLAM (RTAB-MAP) with $0.077~\text{m}$ 2D path error, but full SLAM overhead restricted planning to 2D occupancy grids and quasi-static flight speeds ($\sim 0.1~\text{m/s}$). Low-power NVIDIA Jetson Nano implementations either exhibit higher navigation errors ($0.32~\text{m}$ GPS-degraded MAE and $0.28~\text{m}$ waypoint error in Abdul Rahman et al.~\cite{abdulrahman2025}, which also relies on outdoor GPS fallback) or require supplementary LiDAR sensors alongside stereo cameras ($0.40~\text{m}$ indoor navigation accuracy in James et al.~\cite{james2023}). Moreover, many trajectory planners are benchmarked using external motion-capture ground truth rather than closed-loop onboard VIO feedback, leaving the coupled behavior of VIO drift, real-time 3D voxel mapping, and geometric control uncharacterized on ARM-only hardware.

The present study directly bridges this \emph{Compute--Accuracy--Autonomy} gap. By integrating thread-decoupled stereo-inertial VINS-Fusion (with disabled RGB and IR-emitter streams at $420\times 240$ resolution, $15~\text{Hz}$ stereo-IR input, and $200~\text{Hz}$ IMU pre-integration), ESDF-free 3D B-spline optimization (EGO-Planner), and a $200~\text{Hz}$ $SE(3)$ geometric controller, we achieve complete 3D autonomous obstacle avoidance at $0.5~\text{m/s}$---a fivefold speed increase over prior Raspberry Pi 4B visual navigation~\cite{shah2025}---entirely onboard a GPU-less Raspberry Pi 4 Model B. Validated across both Gazebo SITL simulations ($\text{RMSE}_{3D} = 0.398~\text{m}$) and four unstructured indoor and outdoor physical environments without motion capture or GNSS, the system attains an aggregate real-world mean 3D tracking error of $0.276~\text{m}$ ($\text{RMSE}_{3D} = 0.358~\text{m}$) and $0.203~\text{m}$ closed-loop return drift while operating stably at $90.8\%$ mean CPU utilization and $45.2^\circ\text{C}$, demonstrating that sub-$0.30~\text{m}$ 3D visual-inertial navigation is achievable without GPU acceleration or LiDAR payloads.
